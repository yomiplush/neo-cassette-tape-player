# Cassette sound research

Research date: 2026-11-09. Scope: believable playback hiss, physical controls, and winding; distinguish recorded electrical output from a microphone beside the transport.

## Verified sources and limits

- The requested [YouTube search](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=cassete+tape+noise) was attempted, but retrieval was throttled. No claim here is based on listening to those videos; search results alone are not acoustic evidence or permission to reuse a recording.
- [TEAC 450 instruction manual, printed p. 5](https://bh.hallikainen.org/uploads/harold/Teac450InstructionManual.pdf) identifies tape hiss and amplifier noise, explains that hiss is especially conspicuous above 1 kHz, and documents frequency-dependent Dolby B reduction. This is a manufacturer primary document hosted by an archive. It supports broadband, treble-weighted noise, not a fixed audible tone.
- [TASCAM 202MKVII manufacturer manual](https://tascam.jp/downloads/products/tascam/202mkvii/202MK7_OM_EFS_vB.pdf) specifies 0.25% WRMS wow/flutter. This is a machine-specific weighted measurement, not a universal cassette target or a direct sine-LFO amplitude specification.
- [stecman: Automatic tapedeck rewind, fastforward, play](https://freesound.org/people/stecman/sounds/376058/) is an original recording of electromechanical operation; the creator describes digital controls engaging the head and automatic end-of-tape stopping. Metadata: 32.218 s, stereo, 96 kHz/24-bit original; explicitly CC0. The author also confirms recording it personally in December 2016. Public [MP3 preview](https://cdn.freesound.org/previews/376/376058_1595545-hq.mp3) is suitable for inspection; original downloads require login. Preview codec properties differ from the original.
- [Ornitorrinco: Rewinding Tape Recorder](https://freesound.org/people/Ornitorrinco/sounds/329715/) explicitly describes a low-battery **mini-cassette** recorder recorded with a Zoom 6: 24.450 s, stereo, 96 kHz/24-bit; explicitly CC0. Useful as a worn-machine alternative, not evidence that healthy compact-cassette transports should squeal.
- [Joseph Sardin: Cassette Player, Rewind and Stop](https://bigsoundbank.com/cassette-player-rewind-and-stop-s0566.html) describes two rewind/stop actions: 8 s, mono, 48 kHz/16-bit, studio. Its creator explicitly permits modification and redistribution in apps under CC0.
- [Spring Spring: Tape Recorder Opening and Closing](https://opengameart.org/content/tape-recorder-opening-and-closing-sound-effects) provides separate hatch WAVs and explicitly labels them CC0. These are hatch references; do not relabel them as play-button recordings without inspection.

## Implementation recommendations (inference/design choices)

1. Keep electrical tape hiss and physical transport sounds on separate gains. A microphone recording of a rewind mechanism does not establish how much of that sound should be heard through headphones. Let playback hiss be quiet, continuous, broadband and treble weighted; tune against music and silence. Do not create audible cyclical amplitude breathing merely to signal “analog.”
2. Replace the dominant fixed saw/sine winding stack with a short licensed real transport recording where practical. Preserve onset acceleration, irregular body vibration, and the release/stop transient. For long holds, crossfade multiple interior regions; do not loop an obvious control clunk or a single repeated pulse. Use original one-shot boundaries for controls; pitch variation should be small enough to preserve their physical identity.
3. If synthesis is retained, use filtered stochastic noise as the winding body, several weak broadened resonances, and slowly varying nonperiodic amplitude/speed. Fixed 465/980 Hz sine peaks and a regular 17.5 Hz gate are design artifacts unless a real reference establishes them. Avoid making the motor a foreground musical pitch.
4. Wow/flutter should modulate the program's timing/pitch, not add a whistle. Combine subtle slow drift and irregular faster variation; reserve obvious pitch instability for high wear. Verify a sustained test note and music: regular dual-LFO vibrato should not dominate. Do not equate the TASCAM weighted spec directly with delay depth.
5. Distinguish press/latch, mechanism engagement, steady running, and stop/release. A bounded transient followed by continuous mechanical texture communicates operation more convincingly than one generic synthetic click on every control. Let winding fade naturally on release; rapidly fade program/hiss as the transport changes state to avoid clicks.

These are implementable starting points, not measured universal spectra. Listening and waveform/spectral inspection of the selected reference must decide exact gains, cut points and loop regions. No YouTube recording is licensed or copied by this research. Keep source URL, author, CC0 declaration, download date and edits alongside any shipped sample, even though CC0 does not require attribution.

## Asset processing and measured observations

The shipped recordings were inspected numerically using decoded mono 44.1 kHz PCM, RMS/peak windows and FFT, rather than a claimed listening comparison. In stecman's recording, steady 2–4 s windows measured approximately −49.3 dBFS RMS with a strong component near 341 Hz; 9–24 s windows were roughly −54.6 to −53.1 dBFS with components near 360/374 Hz. Engagement/release transients were much stronger (approximately −10 to −14 dBFS peak). Sardin's steady rewind regions were around −56/−57 dBFS RMS with a 328–336 Hz component, while stop transients approached 0 dBFS. These are properties of these microphone recordings, not universal deck specifications.

`scripts/prepare-mechanisms.py` separates steady sections from transients, applies 65 Hz high-pass/9500 Hz low-pass, normalizes them independently, and wraps winding loops with a 120 ms equal-power crossfade. The manifest pins source and output hashes. Assigning the two regions to forward/reverse is an interface design inference, not verified identification of the original transport direction. The application varies loop rate slightly with spool position; this too is an illustrative design choice.

The playback bus keeps treble-weighted hiss, modest wow/flutter and irregular shallow wear dropouts separate from the mechanical recordings. Synthetic filtered noise remains available if recording fetches fail. No YouTube audio is redistributed; practical listening on physical phones remains outside the automated numerical/browser verification.
